Education & Training

II

My academic path has spanned three countries and two language families — from undergraduate training in Saudi Arabia, through graduate work in Pakistan on Arabic NLP, to my current position in Denmark working on Scandinavian languages. The throughline has been a sustained interest in building computational tools for languages that are historically or resourcefully under-served.

2016 — 2021 UET · Pakistan

Doctor of Philosophy in Computer Science

University of Engineering and Technology, Lahore · Pakistan

Thesis supervisor: Prof. Mohammad Shahbaz

"A Hybrid Approach for Sentiment Analysis of Arabic Text"

The dissertation developed a hybrid framework combining machine learning and rule-based techniques for sentiment analysis of Arabic text — addressing the morphological richness and dialectal variation that make Arabic NLP particularly challenging.

Section · III

Research Interests

III

My research bridges natural language processing and the digital humanities. The animating question is how computational methods can be made sensitive to the linguistic, historical, and cultural specificity of the texts they analyse — whether historical Scandinavian novels from the 19th century or contemporary Arabic discourse on social media. Much of my work involves developing, fine-tuning, and evaluating language models for languages and time periods that off-the-shelf tools handle poorly.

Area · 01

Natural Language Processing

Sentiment and emotion analysis, text classification, summarisation, and topic modelling — applied to both contemporary Arabic text and historical Scandinavian literature. A recurring theme is building tools that respect the linguistic specifics of low-resource and morphologically rich languages.

sentiment summarisation classification topic modelling
Area · 02

Machine & Deep Learning

Designing and fine-tuning Transformer-based large language models for historical and low-resource languages. Working with CNNs, RNNs, and modern LLM architectures for text analysis tasks, with attention to reproducibility and evaluation methodology.

transformers LLMs fine-tuning pytorch
Area · 03

Computational Social Sciences

Using NLP to track literary and social change over time — detecting sentiment shifts, thematic evolution, and euphemism patterns in historical texts. The MiMe/MeMo project exemplifies this approach, tracing modernity in Scandinavian literature 1870–1900.

literary analysis social change historical NLP cultural analytics
Area · 04

Computational Linguistics

Development and evaluation of pre-trained language models for historical Danish and Norwegian. Working with historical text variants, orthographic variation, and the challenges of applying contemporary models to century-old prose.

historical Danish historical Norwegian language models evaluation
Area · 05

Digital Humanities

Computational approaches to humanities research questions — from noise detection in literary corpora to euphemism detection in death discourse. Building bridges between computer science methods and humanities scholarship, with an emphasis on interpretability.

corpus analysis euphemism detection noise detection interpretability
Area · 06

Explainable AI for Computational Law

My current focus under the XHAILe project: building hybrid AI systems for legal applications that combine the accuracy of neural methods with the transparency required in legal reasoning. Developing accurate and explainable legal chatbots that can be trusted in professional settings.

legal AI explainability chatbots hybrid systems

Computational methods in the humanities should be measured not by the benchmarks they top, but by whether they illuminate something about a text, a period, or a language that a careful reader alone could not see.

— Research statement, 2026

Teaching & Pedagogy

V

On teaching

My teaching spans computer science, digital humanities, and language technology — from undergraduate programming to PhD-level courses on the treatment of language data. I believe students learn best when they engage with real, messy data and build systems that operate on texts they find intellectually compelling. My courses at Copenhagen connect technical NLP methods to humanities research questions, drawing students from both computer science and the humanities.
